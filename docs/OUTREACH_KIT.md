## Technical-community version

I’m recruiting participants for the first pilot of Reasoning Diff Lab, a reviewer-governed semantic diff over two frozen reasoning graphs.

The engine and included fixtures are internally verified, but no empirical usefulness claim exists. The pilot compares structured review against a prose-only baseline and records entry burden, reviewer time, useful findings, misleading findings, missed divergences, matcher decisions, and reuse intent.

Need: two analysts and one reviewer, roughly 90–120 minutes, synthetic evidence, local execution. Scheduling begins only after the v2.0.1 safety build and cold-machine rehearsal pass.

## Reply when someone volunteers

Thanks for volunteering. Please do not post contact details, schedules, private case material, or personal information in this public issue.

Before scheduling, I’ll privately confirm your role, availability, device setup, and whether you inspected the pilot fixture reference paths or design notes. The session uses synthetic evidence and the final verified `release/v2.0.1` build.

## Reply when someone asks whether it works

The deterministic software behavior is tested against included fixtures, but usefulness and human burden have not been established. The pilot is designed to test whether the surfaced divergences are accurate, useful, non-obvious, complete enough, and worth the structured-entry cost.

## Reply when someone wants to use a real case

The first pilot uses only the included synthetic cases. Real cases introduce privacy, authorization, retention, and ecological-validity issues that this session is not designed to handle.

## Do not claim

Avoid saying:

- the tool improves investigations;
- the tool detects bad reasoning;
- the tool determines which analyst is correct;
- the tool has been validated;
- the pilot will prove effectiveness;
- the taxonomy captures how people actually think;
- 65 automated tests demonstrate usability or scientific validity;
- v2.0.1 is ready before CI and cold-machine rehearsal pass.

Accurate statement:

> The implementation has strong internal verification and a corrected pilot-safety candidate. The workflow hypothesis remains empirically unvalidated, and the human session remains behind a final verification gate.
